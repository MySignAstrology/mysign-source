Thesis

Not an astrology chatbot

Nine companies already shipped astrology-with-a-chatbox. Co–Star's Void, Nebula, AstroGPT, Nummi, half a dozen Vedic apps. They are all stateless: every conversation starts from zero, and the answer you get is one a free ChatGPT session would also give you.

My Sign is a daily emotional companion whose lens happens to be astrology. The astrology is the personality and the trust mechanism — it's why she opens it instead of a journal app, and why the advice feels like it comes from somewhere rather than from a model. The retention comes from somewhere else entirely: it remembers her life.

Everything in this spec serves one loop. Push in the morning → a fifteen-second check-in → a real conversation → a silent write to memory that makes tomorrow's push better. Ship that loop and nothing else. Every feature that doesn't feed it is v2.

The five gaps, in priority order

  1. Memory. No competitor's chat knows you asked about the same person three weeks ago. This is an engineering advantage, not a content one — which means it's defensible against a company whose moat is astrologers.
  2. It asks first. Mood has to be an input to the conversation, not a separate journal tab. AstroMood has all the pieces and keeps them in different rooms.
  3. Unprompted. The 7:30am notification is the product. The chat is what happens when the notification lands right.
  4. Decision-shaped answers. Not horoscope prose — a position, with a reason, and a time. Nebula does this with human psychics at high marginal cost. Nobody does it well with a model.
  5. Voice. Co–Star originally won on tone: blunt, funny, screenshot-able. Its AI features lost that. A chat that sounds like a friend rather than a fortune cookie is a real moat with this demographic.
Core loop

One day in the product

  1. 07:30

    The read

    A push derived from today's actual transits to her natal chart. Not a horoscope. One sentence, specific, and it names the mechanism so the claim is falsifiable rather than vague.

    Push

    Moon's in your 12th until Thursday. You're going to want to cancel everything — that's the transit, not you.

  2. 07:31

    The check-in

    Opening the push lands directly on a fifteen-second check-in — one mood dial, one optional line of "what's going on." Never a blank journal page. This is the input that makes the rest of the day's conversation about her instead of about her sign.

  3. All day

    The conversation

    Chat opens pre-loaded with today's transit facts, her check-in, and the retrieved slice of her history. She asks the real question.

    Her

    ok so should i answer him or not

    The app

    Mercury goes direct Thursday. Both times you've texted him mid-retrograde you told me the next day you hated how it came out. Wait two days. If you still want to say it, you'll say it better.

  4. Nightly

    The write

    A background pass extracts durable facts from the day and appends them to memory — who she mentioned, what she decided, how she felt, and the sky state at the time. She never sees this run. It is the entire reason tomorrow is better than today.

Memory

The part that's actually hard

Three stores, each with a different write cadence. Keep them separate — collapsing them into one vector blob is the mistake that makes these products feel like goldfish.

PROFILE slow-changing, user-visible, editable birth data date, time, place, time-confidence natal chart computed once, cached forever people name, relation, their chart if known themes recurring concerns she has named herself EPISODES append-only, one per check-in or session date, mood, free text questions asked, decision reached sky_state transits + aspects at time of writing PATTERNS derived weekly, never asserted without data claim "mood dips 2-3 days before the new moon" support "4 of the last 5 cycles" confidence low | medium | high

Retrieval, per turn

Profile in full, the last seven days of episodes verbatim, a semantic match against older episodes on the current question, and any pattern whose trigger fires on today's sky. That's the whole context recipe.

The rule that makes it feel like magic instead of a horoscope

Never assert a trait from her sign. Only assert a pattern from her data.

Never

Cancers are naturally nostalgic — it's your water sign nature.

Always

You've told me this three times now, and all three were within a day of the Moon hitting your natal Moon. It's a monthly thing for you.

The second one is unfalsifiable-sounding but literally true and checkable, which is exactly why it lands. It's also something no competitor can say, because none of them kept the data.

Engine

Real sky, not vibes

The astrology-literate user is your best subscriber and she will detect a GPT wrapper within two messages. The fix is architectural: the model never computes or invents a planetary position. Every claim it makes about the sky traces to a row in a table you computed.

  • Ephemeris. Swiss Ephemeris via pyswisseph. Real positions, not an API you can't audit.
  • Natal chart from date, time, and place. Time matters enormously — if she doesn't know her birth time, degrade to a solar chart and say so in the UI. Silently faking houses is how you lose the users who care.
  • Daily computation: transiting positions, aspects to natal points (conjunction, opposition, square, trine, sextile, orb ≤ 3°), moon phase, moon sign, house ingresses, retrograde status. Cached per user per day.
  • Interpretation corpus. A curated body of interpretations keyed by placement / aspect / house, retrieved and handed to the model as source material. This is where you hire an actual astrologer, and it's the second moat after memory.

The model's job is synthesis and voice. The facts and the interpretations are both retrieved. That constraint is what makes the output specific rather than plausible.

Calibration

Two numbers that came out of building it

The salience scorer and the pattern verifier are written and running against real ephemeris data. Both produced results that change the spec.

A third of days are quiet, and that had to be measured

Salience is scored in code — aspect tightness, whether the natal point is personal, calendar events inside the window — because a model asked whether today is interesting always says yes. The non-obvious part is novelty decay: Pluto sits within one degree of a natal point for six weeks, so scoring on orb alone marks forty straight days as significant. Slow transits now halve in score each time the app actually surfaces them, and re-sharpen on their exact date.

Thresholds are calibrated to the 85th and 40th percentiles of a full year of one chart's daily scores rather than picked by hand. The resulting mix:

BandDays/yrShareBehavior
High4913%Lead with the tightest item. Up to four sentences.
Medium16946%Something worth saying, kept light. Three sentences.
Low14740%Say nothing is happening. Two sentences, and "quiet day" is a complete answer.

Recalibrate per user from a rolling 90-day window once they have one. A chart dense with tight natal aspects is busy every day, and a global constant would either shout constantly or never speak.

You cannot honestly show her a pattern for three months

The pattern job has the model propose testable predicates and Python verify them — three gates: support, lift over her own base rate, and a Bonferroni-corrected binomial test. That last gate is the one that is easy to skip and the one that matters. Test five candidate patterns against thirty noisy days and one will look convincing by chance.

Run against synthetic histories with one true rule planted and three decoys, the detection threshold is unambiguous:

HistoryTrue patternDecoys kept
30 daysMissed0 of 3
60 daysMissed0 of 3
90 daysFound — 8 of 8, lift 2.310 of 3
240 daysFound — 22 of 23, lift 2.390 of 3

Before the correction was in, a decoy survived at thirty days — "you feel worse around the full moon," 3 of 4, lift 1.88. Plausible, quotable, and false. That is precisely the claim that would have shipped.

Product consequence: the memory moment at week two is episode recall — quoting her own words back with a date. That works immediately. Pattern claims are a month-three feature and should not appear in onboarding copy, screenshots, or the App Store listing as though they are available on day one.

Retrieval by recency alone loses the best line

The strongest output in the voice test cited episodes from 24 and 11 days back — both outside any sensible recency window. A seven-day verbatim window silently drops exactly the callbacks that make the product feel like itself. Retrieval pulls on named people and topics across the full history, and needs embeddings before launch.

Voice

Write the persona before the code

This is the highest-leverage artifact in the whole project and it costs nothing to test. Specific, warm, a little blunt. Names the mechanism. Takes a position. Never hedges into uselessness.

Wrong

Only you can know what's right for you! Trust your intuition and the universe will guide you. ✨

Right

Venus is retrograde in your 7th until the 14th. This is the worst two weeks of the year for you to make a call about him. Sit on it.

Hard constraints on the persona

  • No therapy-speak. No "I hear you," no "that sounds really hard."
  • No cosmic platitudes. "The universe has a plan" is a banned phrase.
  • Take a position when asked for one. "It depends" is a failure state.
  • Emoji sparingly or not at all — Co–Star's original voice used none and it read as more serious, not less fun.
  • Short. Two to four sentences is the target; long paragraphs read as a horoscope.
Safety

The thing that gets you pulled from the App Store

You are building an emotionally intimate persona for a young, female-skewing audience, and telling it to give decisive advice. Guardrails are a day-one requirement, not a v2 item. Classify every inbound message before the astrology layer runs.

Green Everyday

Relationships, work, mood, friends, plans. Answer normally, in full voice, with a position.

Amber High-stakes and irreversible

Leaving a partner, quitting a job, moving, money, cutting off family. Still engage — but the model must not deliver a verdict. It reflects, names the transit, and states plainly that the decision is hers. No astrological determinism on anything she can't undo.

Red Crisis and clinical

Self-harm, abuse, disordered eating, medication, medical symptoms. Drop the persona entirely. No transit framing, ever. Surface real crisis resources, offer to keep talking plainly, and log for human review.

Standing rules

  • No medical, legal, or financial advice, in any voice, under any framing.
  • No death, illness, or pregnancy predictions.
  • No "your chart says you'll never" — closed statements about her future are out of bounds.
  • Age-rate honestly (17+), disclose in onboarding that this is for reflection and not prediction, and keep engagement mechanics off the lonely-user pattern.
  • Every amber and red event logged and sampled weekly by a human. Budget for this.

Stack

Concrete recommendations

Client

Expo / React Native. Faster to v1, and you don't need native until widgets and Live Activities — which are v1.1+ anyway.

Backend

Python on FastAPI, because pyswisseph is Python. On AWS this is Lambda plus DynamoDB — chart computation is a textbook Lambda workload, and you already run this stack.

Model

Claude Sonnet 5 for chat volume, Opus 5 for the nightly extraction and weekly pattern job where quality matters more than cost. Long system prompt, heavy retrieved context.

Memory store

DynamoDB for profile and episodes, a small vector index for semantic recall over older episodes. Don't over-engineer this before you have a thousand users.

Business

Pricing and the numbers to hit

$9.99/mo or $59.99/yr — under CHANI, above Co–Star's Pro-Star, which is the right place for a product that does more than either.

Paywall the conversation, not the ritual. Free tier keeps the morning push, the check-in, and three chat messages a day. The ritual is what builds the habit; the habit is what makes the paywall convert. Gating the push would be the single most expensive mistake available here.

Validate

Three tests before you write app code

All three are runnable in under two weeks and none of them need a build. If any fails, the spec above is wrong and cheap to change.

  1. Test 1

    Does the voice work? — Passed

    Eight hand-run conversations against a real computed chart and the v0.1 system prompt. Seven passed. The one failure was the quiet-day case, and it is now fixed in code rather than in prose. Still outstanding: show the transcript to five people in the target demo — the test proves the voice is specific, not that it is likeable.

  2. Test 2

    Is memory magic or is it creepy?

    Ten people, fourteen days, over SMS or Telegram with a human in the loop. Watch for the week-two moment where it references something from week one. Does she light up or recoil? This is the single biggest unknown in the product.

  3. Test 3

    Will she switch?

    Twenty people who already pay for an astrology app. Show them the concept. Would they pay $10/mo for this instead? Switching cost in this category is near zero, which cuts both ways.
