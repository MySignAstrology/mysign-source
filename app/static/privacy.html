Privacy

What we keep, and why.

Last updated 27 November 2026

My Sign asks for the moment you were born and then holds what you tell it about your life. That is a lot to trust something with. This page says exactly what happens to it, in plain language, and every sentence here is meant to describe something the app actually does. If one stops being true, the page is wrong and we will fix it.

Who we are. My Sign is operated by XII Studios LLC, Sunny Isles Beach, Florida, USA, which is the controller of your data. Questions about this page go to support@mysignapp.com.

What we collect

  • Your email address — to sign you in. We send a six-digit code; there is no password.
  • Payment details, if you subscribe — you enter your card on Stripe's page, not ours, and we never see the full number. We keep your plan, its dates and Stripe's reference for you.
  • Purchases in the iPhone app. If you subscribe in our iPhone app, Apple takes the payment through your Apple Account. We never receive your card, billing address or Apple Account email. Apple sends us a signed record of the purchase: which plan, its start, renewal and end dates, whether it is a trial or was refunded, Apple's transaction reference, and a random identifier we created to link the purchase to your My Sign account. We use it only to give you Plus and keep it in step with your subscription. We delete it when you delete your account. Deleting your account does not cancel an Apple subscription: cancel it in iOS Settings → Apple Account → Subscriptions.
  • Notifications in the iPhone app. If you turn on notifications in the iPhone app, the app sends us a device token: an identifier Apple gives your phone for this app. We keep it with your account and send it to Apple's push notification service, together with the text of each notification, so that Apple can deliver it. We use it for nothing else. Turning notifications off in the app, signing out or deleting your account removes the token from our servers.
  • Your phone number, if you choose to use one — only to text you sign-in codes. We never use it for marketing, never text you anything else, and never share it. Replying STOP stops our texts.
  • Your name, birth date, birth time and birth city — to compute your chart. If you skip the time we say so on the reading rather than guess.
  • The free reading on our homepage — the birth date, time and city you type there are sent to us to compute that reading and are not saved or logged. They stay in that browser tab so you don't have to retype them if you go on to sign up, and are gone when you close it.
  • The compatibility check at /check — the two birthdays you type there are sent to us to compute the score and are not saved or logged. A link or card you share carries the first names you typed and the result, never a birth date. If you tap to go on to sign up, what you typed stays in that browser tab so we can add that person to your account, and it is gone when you close it.
  • Your timezone and country — your timezone comes from your browser, so "today" and the morning notification follow where you are. Your country decides which crisis line you are shown.
  • About you, if you fill it in — the hobbies, interests, skills and passions you pick or type in, and one optional line about something you'd do even if nobody paid you. It is used to make your replies more specific to your life, and to show you how your days compare with the elements in your chart. Change or clear it any time in Settings → About you.
  • Daily check-ins — a mood number from 1 to 10 and, if you add one, a line of text.
  • Your conversations — everything you type and everything the app replies, including messages sent at a difficult moment.
  • What the app remembers — at the end of each day, a short summary of that day, a few of your exact words worth keeping, the names and topics that came up, and the mood. Once there is enough history, it also keeps patterns it has checked against your days. Your questions are tagged by topic so it can notice what you keep coming back to.
  • Dated life events — only if you add them to narrow down an unknown birth time.
  • Other people — see Other people's details and Linking with someone, below.
  • A notification subscription — only if you turn on the morning notification. It is a device address and encryption keys issued by your browser or phone.
  • Replies you report — if you flag a reply as wrong or hurtful, we keep which reply and the reason you give. A report also lets a member of the team read your recent conversation, to look into it.
  • Which screens you open and which buttons you tap — so we can see where the app is confusing and fix it. Each event is the name of a screen or a control (for example "People tab" or "Check in"), the time, and a few fixed values such as the mood number you picked or which kind of relationship you chose. It also includes how long the app is open in front of you (a short signal every 30 seconds while you are using it, with the screen you are on), whether you opened the app from a notification and which kind it was, and when your plan starts, renews or ends. It never includes anything you type: not your messages, check-in notes, names or birth details. It is kept in our own database and in our own analytics storage at AWS, under your account ID; no analytics company receives it. You can switch it off in Settings → Help improve the app, which stops it at once and deletes what was kept (see Your controls).
  • Visits to our website and the screens before you sign in — counted without an account: the page or screen, the time, your country (from the network, not your location), the kind of device and the family of browser and operating system (for example "mobile, iOS, Safari"), the site that linked to us (its domain only, never the full address), and any campaign tag in the link you followed. To tell one visit from the next, your browser keeps a random number we generate, in its own storage rather than a cookie, but only if you say yes on the website's banner; until then we count the visit and its taps with no number attached. Details and the switch are on the cookies page. It is not tied to your name, email or account unless you sign in on that device with Help improve the app on. We do not store your IP address or your browser's full identification string. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, we keep only the anonymous count and drop the random number.
  • Usage bookkeeping — how many messages you have sent today (for the free-plan and safety limits), whether a reply referred back to something you said and how quickly you answered it, and which daily lines you have already seen so they are not repeated. Sign-ups and returns are also counted as daily totals that contain no names or IDs.

We do not collect your location, contacts, photos, or advertising identifiers. The app sets one cookie that keeps you signed in, and a short-lived one while you sign in (see Sign-in codes, below). It does not use analytics or tracking cookies, and no third-party analytics or advertising code runs in the app or on the website. The cookies page lists what we keep in your browser.

What we do with it

Your chart and your conversations are the product. The app reads today's sky against your birth chart, remembers what you have told it, and uses both to answer you. Memory is the point: the app keeps what you say so that it can refer back to it later. That is stated on the consent screen because it is unusual and you should choose it deliberately.

We use your data to run the app for you and to keep it safe. We do not use it for advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not use it to build a profile of you for anyone else.

Email. We email you sign-in codes and messages about your subscription, such as the reminder before a trial ends, and Stripe sends your receipts. We do not send marketing email. If we ever start, it will only go to people who agreed, and every message will have an unsubscribe link.

The AI we use, and what it sees

My Sign's replies, readings and memory are written by a third-party AI model: Claude, made by Anthropic, PBC. To do that, the app sends some of your personal data to Anthropic over its commercial API. Here is exactly what goes, and when:

  • When you send a message: the message itself, roughly your last ten messages and replies, today's check-in (mood and text), your chart facts and today's sky, short summaries and a few of your own quoted words from recent and related days, the names and relationships of people you have mentioned or saved, and their compatibility scores, and what you have told us under About you (your hobbies, interests, skills and passions). If the AI needs more, it can look up your chart, the sky on another date, a saved person's compatibility report, or your past days.
  • Before that, a safety check: the text of your message, on its own, is sent to the same model to judge whether it needs a careful or crisis response. If a message clearly signals crisis, the app answers with a crisis line straight away. In that case the message is not sent to the AI for a reply.
  • Once a day: the previous day's whole conversation and check-in are sent, so the AI can write that day's memory.
  • Once a week, once you have ten or more days: your saved daily memories are sent so the AI can suggest patterns. Our own code then checks each one against your days before you see it.
  • Once per reading: your first name and chart facts for your birth-chart reading, and a saved person's name and computed compatibility for their written reading.
  • Morning notification: by default the morning line is chosen from lines we wrote, with no AI involved. If you switch to the sky-based notification, the same kind of context as a chat message is sent each morning to write it.

Never sent to Anthropic: your email address, your phone number, your account ID, your notification subscription, or your raw birth date, time and place. Only the computed chart is sent.

What Anthropic does with it. Anthropic processes this data to produce the response and under its commercial terms. By default it does not use API data to train its models. It deletes API inputs and outputs from its systems within 30 days. The exception is a request its automated systems flag as violating its usage policy: those inputs and outputs can be kept for up to 2 years. See Anthropic's privacy center: how long data is stored and whether data is used for training.

Before the app stores or sends anything about your day, it asks you to tick a box that names Anthropic and agrees to this. If you do not want your data sent to Anthropic, do not use the chat, readings or memory. Those are the features that need it, and without them the app cannot work. You can delete everything at any time (see Your controls).

Who else sees it

  • Amazon Web Services stores and runs everything, in the US (Ohio, us-east-2): the database, the servers, backups, logs, usage analytics, and sign-in emails and texts, which go through Amazon SES and AWS End User Messaging. Your mobile carrier also handles any text we send you. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  • Anthropic, as described above.
  • Stripe takes web payments. It receives your email address, your card details, what you bought and your account ID, and handles them under its own privacy policy. It does not receive your chart or anything you write.
  • Apple takes payments made in the iPhone app and handles them under its own privacy policy. It does not receive your chart or anything you write.
  • Apple (Apple Push Notification service) delivers notifications to the iPhone app. It receives your device token and the text of each notification, for delivery.
  • Apple (speech recognition). If you tap the orb and speak, Apple’s speech recognition turns what you say into text, under Apple’s own privacy policy. We receive only that text, handled exactly like a typed message. We never receive or keep the recording.
  • ElevenLabs gives the reader her voice. When she reads a reply aloud, the words of that line are sent to ElevenLabs to turn them into audio. It receives those words and nothing else from your account: not your name, birth details or chart. When the studio voice is off, your phone’s own voice is used and nothing is sent.
  • Shopify runs checkout for the shop at shop.mysignapp.com and hosts its product photos, so it sees your network address when you browse the shop, and your order details if you buy. Nothing from your account or chart is sent to it.
  • Amazon Location Service. If a birthplace isn't in our own list and you tap to search further, the place name you typed is sent to this AWS service, which uses map data from HERE. Your name and birth date are not included.
  • A person, when a message suggests someone may be in danger. The app replies with a crisis line for your country. At the same moment it alerts the on-call member of our team through PagerDuty , an incident-alerting service. The alert contains your account ID and the first 120 characters of that message, so a human can judge quickly how serious it is. That person may then read your conversation. We do this because missing someone in danger would be worse. It is not an emergency service, and nobody will contact emergency services on your behalf.
  • A member of the team, only in these cases. Our team does not read conversations as a matter of routine. A person may read yours after a crisis alert (above), if you tell the app you are under 18, or if you report a reply. Reporting opens your recent conversation, not only that reply. Our staff console refuses to open any other conversation, and every attempt is recorded.
  • Notification services. If you turn on notifications, the morning message is delivered through your device's push service: Apple, Google, Mozilla or Microsoft, depending on your device and browser. The message is encrypted end to end, so they cannot read it. They do see that a notification was delivered.
  • When you email us, the message is stored by AWS for 90 days and forwarded to the team's inbox (currently Gmail), where it is kept for as long as we need it to help you.

Nobody else sees it. No advertising, no data brokers, no analytics service that can see what you write. We do not sell data and we do not share it with anyone not listed above, except where the law requires us to.

Other people's details

When you compare charts with someone, you give us their name, how they relate to you, and their birth date, place and, if you know it, time. We use it only to compute the comparison for you. Their computed compatibility and their name may be sent to Anthropic when you ask about them, but their raw birth details are not. We never contact them, and they never become a user.

Please only add someone if you have reason to think they would be comfortable with it. Removing a person deletes their birth details and their compatibility report. Their name will still appear in your conversations and memories until you delete your account. If you are someone who has been added and want your details removed, write to support@mysignapp.com.

Linking with someone

You can invite someone to link with you, or accept someone's invite. A link is between two My Sign accounts, and each person enters their own birth details.

  • What each of you sees. Once you are linked, each of you sees the other's name, birth date, birth time and birth city, the chart computed from them, and the daily compatibility between you. Neither of you ever sees anything the other writes: not messages, not check-in text, not memories.
  • Your mood, only if you say yes for that person. Your daily mood number (1 to 10) and the time you checked in are shown to a linked person only if you said yes for them: when you accept an invite (it starts unticked), or on their card at any time. (Links made before 30 October 2026 follow the Settings switch until you change them on the card.) Share how I'm feeling in Settings turns it off for everyone at once. Accepting an invite never changes that setting.
  • Invite links. A link works for one person, for 7 days. You can see who joined through each of your links, and cancel a link that hasn't been used. Until it is used, anyone holding the link sees only your first name.
  • Unlinking. Removing a linked person from your People unlinks you both. They lose your birth details, your chart, your mood, and everything they held that was computed from your chart (the compatibility report, any shared reveal), and you lose theirs. Their own notes about how it was going with you are theirs; what they wrote about you in a conversation stays in their account, as anything they write does.
  • If either of you deletes your account, the link and everything above go the same way, and your invite links are deleted.

What our own logs contain

Our operational logs record the shape of what happens, and never the content. A log line says that a message was sent, how long it was, and whether it was routine or urgent. Log lines identify you only by an opaque account ID. The logging layer is built to replace message text, email addresses, phone numbers and birth details with a character count, and an automated test fails if a message body reaches a log line.

How long we keep it

  • While your account exists: everything listed above, so that memory works. That includes your conversations, check-ins and daily memories, with no expiry.
  • Sign-in codes: they work for 15 minutes and only once. The record of the code is erased within about an hour, and the database itself may take up to 48 hours more to sweep it away. To stop abuse we count how many codes are sent to and tried for each address or number, from each network address, and to each email domain, and we set a random cookie on the device that asked for a code so its tries can be told apart from tries made elsewhere. Those counts hold only a scrambled form of the address, number or domain and are erased within eight days; the cookie lasts a day.
  • Screens, taps and website visits: 90 days, then deleted automatically. After that we keep only daily totals (for example "120 people opened Today"), which contain no account ID or random number.
  • Short-lived bookkeeping: message counts and the day's line are kept for a few days.
  • Session cookie: 90 days, or until you sign out.
  • Operational logs: 90 days. Request traces: 30 days.
  • Support emails: 90 days in our mail storage, plus the forwarded copy in the team's inbox.
  • Anthropic: within 30 days, or up to 2 years if flagged for a usage-policy violation (above).
  • When you delete your account: every record is removed from our database immediately. That covers your chart, conversations, check-ins, memories, the people you added, your notification subscription, your sign-in details and your invite links. It also removes you from the People of everyone you were linked with, together with everything they held that was computed from your chart. Screens and taps already copied to our analytics storage are not removed one by one; they carry only your account ID, which no longer leads anywhere once the account is gone, and they are deleted on the 90-day schedule above.
  • After deletion: encrypted database backups exist for disaster recovery and are retained for up to 35 days. Nothing is restored from them except to recover from a failure affecting everyone, and never to recover an individual account you asked us to delete. Logs (which hold your account ID but not your words) age out on the 90-day schedule above. Anything already sent to Anthropic or PagerDuty follows their retention, above.

Your controls

  • Download your data. Settings → Download your data downloads, as a file, your account details, chart, conversations, check-ins, memories, people and everything else we hold under your account. Notification keys are left out because they are device secrets, and so are values we can recompute from your chart.
  • Delete everything. Settings → Delete everything. It takes effect immediately, and it asks you twice. There is no way yet to delete a single message or a single day. Account deletion is the control.
  • Correct things. Add or change your birth time, your country and your astrology tradition in Settings, and remove anyone you added. For anything else, write to us.
  • Stop sharing how you use the app. Settings → Help improve the app. Turning it off stops the record at once and deletes the copy in our database straight away; the copy in our analytics storage is deleted automatically within 90 days. Your browser's Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track setting is honoured too.
  • Turn notifications off. Settings, or your phone's own controls.
  • Ask a person. support@mysignapp.com. You can ask what we hold, ask us to correct or delete it, or object to how we use it. Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to complain to your data-protection authority.

Depending on where you live

  • California and other US states with privacy laws. We do not sell your personal information or share it for targeted advertising. You can ask to know, correct or delete what we hold, and we will not treat you differently for asking.
  • The EU, UK and similar. We rely on your consent for chat, memory and anything sensitive you choose to tell us, on our contract with you to run the account and take payment, and on our legitimate interest in keeping the service safe. You can withdraw consent by deleting your account. Your data is stored and processed in the United States, including by the providers listed above.

Age

My Sign is for people 18 and over and is not directed at children. We check your birth date at signup and on the free reading, and refuse anyone below that age. If you tell the app you are under 18, chat stops, you are offered the delete button, and the on-call member of our team is alerted with your account ID and may read the conversation. If signup is refused because of your age, the sign-in address you entered stays on file until you ask us to delete it. If you think a child has given us their details, write to support@mysignapp.com and we will delete them.

What My Sign is not

My Sign is for reflection, not prediction. It is not a medical, legal, or financial service, and it is not a crisis service. If a conversation suggests you may be in danger, the app stops and gives you a real crisis line for your country. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

Third-party data in the app

  • Planetary positions are computed with the Swiss Ephemeris by Astrodienst AG, used under the AGPL licence. The calculation runs on our servers; nothing is sent to Astrodienst.
  • Birthplace lookup uses data from GeoNames (geonames.org), licensed CC BY 4.0. It is bundled with the app; nothing is sent to GeoNames.
